How it's calculated, sources, and caveats
Line. Each Cα line has integrated optical depth ∫τdν (Hz), scaled as (ν/60 MHz)γ. Its shape is a Voigt profile: Doppler FWHM ν·Δv/c, plus Lorentzian pressure (∝ν−1.47) and radiation (∝ν−1.93) broadening. The optical depth seen in one channel is ∫τdν times the profile averaged over a channel of width Δν centred on the line.
Signal. Tline = fc · τchan · Tsky (absorption, τ ≪ 1), with Tsky = T66(ν/66)β · e−(νt/ν)2.1 (free-free turnover, on by default with νt = 4 MHz so the sky peaks near 3.7 MHz, inside the ~3–5 MHz downturn seen in space-based measurements; Cong et al. 2021 summarize them). The screen is treated as cold, so its own emission is ignored, and the turnover is the same in every direction even though absorption is stronger along the Galactic plane.
Noise. σT = (Tsky + Trx/η) / √(Δν · t · Nlines). With Trx = 0 and fc = 1 the SNR is τchan√(Δν·t·N), independent of sky brightness and collecting area.
Pointing. With "Scale with distance from the GC" on, the sky at 66 MHz is Toff + (TGC − Toff)·Gc(ℓ, b) and the line strength is ∫τGC·Gl(ℓ, b), where each G is a product of Gaussians in ℓ and b with the FWHMs under Advanced physics. The line ℓ-width default follows Erickson et al. (1995), who detected carbon absorption at every position within |ℓ| ≲ 20° at 76 MHz. Kantharia & Anantharamaiah (2001) report line-to-continuum ratios of a few ×10⁻⁴ to ~10⁻³ at 34.5 MHz across ℓ ≈ 352°–17°, with widths 19–54 km/s. The latitude widths and the continuum profile are assumptions; neither paper gives a falloff law.
Default values. The line inputs default to the best-measured case, toward the GC: ∫τdν = 6.9 Hz and Δv = 31 km/s from the EDA stack (Oonk et al. 2019, n = 461–480); Tsky ≈ 27,000 K at 66 MHz toward the inner plane (Vydula et al. 2023); β = −2.46 (Mozdzen et al. 2019, GC overhead). Instrument defaults are generic: Trx = 0, 6 kHz channels, 3 h. For a sky-average pointing at 1–30 MHz (e.g. a lunar-farside dipole), one set of values that gives ~100 mK at 60 MHz: pointing scaling off, sky 2,850 K, β = −2.55, free-free turnover on at 6 MHz, ∫τdν = 0.35 Hz, Δv = 60 km/s, pressure width 1.8 kHz at 30 MHz, 1 kHz channels.
Caveats. Thermal noise only: bandpass, RFI and continuum-subtraction systematics are not modelled and usually set the real observing time. A line falling between channels can lose up to ~2× more peak at coarse Δν. Noise is assumed to integrate down cleanly across nights. γ is the least constrained input; away from the 60 MHz anchor the result is only as good as γ. Stacking assumes every line in the bandwidth is usable.
